=============================================================================
HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS
=============================================================================
Master R.P. is a 7-year-old previously healthy boy who was referred to pediatric oncology after his pediatrician found leukocytosis with blasts on a CBC obtained for evaluation of fatigue and recurrent fevers. His parents reported a 3-week history of increasing tiredness, loss of appetite, pallor, and development of multiple unexplained bruises on his legs and arms. He had two febrile episodes (39.2 C and 38.7 C) in the past 2 weeks, treated at home with acetaminophen.

He also complained of bone pain, predominantly in his legs and knees, which his parents initially attributed to "growing pains." He had no history of prior illness, hospitalizations, or significant family history of malignancy.

=============================================================================
TUMOR LYSIS SYNDROME PROPHYLAXIS
=============================================================================
Initiated immediately upon admission:
  - IV hydration: 2x maintenance (3,000 mL/m2/day)
  - Rasburicase 0.2 mg/kg IV (rapid uric acid reduction — converted uric acid from 9.4 to 0.6 mg/dL within 4 hours)
  - Allopurinol 100 mg/m2/day PO (maintenance)
  - Cardiac monitoring
  - Electrolytes every 4-6 hours
  No significant tumor lysis syndrome developed.

=============================================================================
TREATMENT: COG AALL1231 PROTOCOL (HIGH RISK B-ALL)
=============================================================================

Phase 1 — Induction (Days 1-29):
  Goal: Achieve complete remission (blast count <5% bone marrow)
  Drugs:
    Vincristine 1.5 mg/m2 IV (Days 1, 8, 15, 22) — max 2 mg
    Dexamethasone 10 mg/m2/day PO (Days 1-7, 15-21)
    PEG-asparaginase 2,500 IU/m2 IM (Day 4) — Erwinia asparaginase alternative planned if hypersensitivity
    Doxorubicin 30 mg/m2 IV (Day 1)
    Triple intrathecal chemotherapy (methotrexate/ARA-C/hydrocortisone): Days 1, 8, 15, 29
    Prophylactic co-trimoxazole for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia prevention

  Induction Toxicities (documented):
    Neutropenic fever (Day 14): Admitted; blood cultures negative; treated empirically with cefepime x 5 days
    Grade 2 nausea/vomiting: Managed with ondansetron and dexamethasone
    Vincristine-related constipation: Managed with lactulose and bowel regimen
    Peripheral neuropathy: Mild tingling in fingers; monitored; resolved
    Mood changes: Steroid-related irritability; counseling provided to family

  Day 29 Bone Marrow Evaluation:
    Blasts: 0.8% by morphology (Complete Remission achieved)
    Minimal Residual Disease (MRD) by flow cytometry: 0.005% (MRD NEGATIVE — threshold <0.01%)
    Significance: MRD negativity at Day 29 is the strongest predictor of long-term remission
    COG Risk Reassignment: STANDARD RISK (due to excellent MRD response despite initial high risk)

Phase 2 — Consolidation (Weeks 5-17):
  Goal: Eliminate residual disease; prevent CNS relapse
  Drugs: High-dose methotrexate 5,000 mg/m2 IV x 4 cycles; leucovorin rescue; 6-mercaptopurine daily; vincristine; intrathecal therapy
  No cranial radiation required (replaced by intrathecal chemotherapy in modern protocols)

  Toxicities during consolidation:
    Methotrexate mucositis: Grade 2; managed with leucovorin rescue and oral care
    Elevated LFTs: Transient; resolved
    Line-associated infection (PICC): Removed and replaced; treated with vancomycin

Phase 3 — Interim Maintenance and Delayed Intensification: Ongoing at time of case writing

Total treatment duration: 2 years (girls) / 3 years (boys)

=============================================================================
DISCUSSION
=============================================================================
Pediatric acute lymphoblastic leukemia is the most common childhood cancer, representing approximately 25% of all pediatric malignancies. With modern risk-stratified chemotherapy protocols, overall survival rates now exceed 90% for pediatric B-ALL.

Key teaching points:
1. The classic presentation triad of pallor, bruising, and lymphadenopathy should prompt immediate CBC.
2. Bone pain in children is a red flag symptom that should not be dismissed as "growing pains."
3. ETV6-RUNX1 translocation (the most common in pediatric ALL) carries an excellent prognosis and responds well to standard chemotherapy without intensification.
4. MRD monitoring at Day 29 is now the gold standard for risk stratification — it predicts relapse risk better than initial clinical features.
5. Cranial radiation has been largely eliminated in modern pediatric ALL protocols, reducing long-term neurocognitive side effects.
6. Long-term survivorship monitoring for late effects (cardiomyopathy, secondary malignancies, endocrine dysfunction, neurocognitive effects) is an essential component of comprehensive care.

OUTCOME AT 6 MONTHS (INTERIM FOLLOW-UP)
