CLINICAL CASE REPORT
Case ID: MED-2024-0521
Title: Acute Ischemic Stroke with Large Vessel Occlusion — Successful Mechanical Thrombectomy in a 67-Year-Old Male
Institution: Comprehensive Stroke Center, Metropolitan University Hospital
Date of Admission: May 3, 2024

=============================================================================
PATIENT INFORMATION
=============================================================================
Age: 67 years
Sex: Male
Occupation: Retired accountant
Chief Complaint: Sudden onset left-sided weakness and speech difficulty at 9:14 AM

=============================================================================
HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS
=============================================================================
Mr. G.F. is a 67-year-old male who was brought to the emergency department by his wife who witnessed sudden onset of left-sided facial droop, left arm and leg weakness, and slurred speech while he was eating breakfast. The patient's wife called 911 immediately. Onset was clearly witnessed at 9:14 AM. EMS arrived within 6 minutes and transported him immediately to the nearest comprehensive stroke center.

Pre-notification was activated, allowing the stroke team to be assembled before patient arrival. EMS transmitted a pre-hospital stroke scale (LAMS score: 4/5, indicating large vessel occlusion).

Last known well: 9:14 AM (witnessed onset — reliable last-known-well time)
ED arrival: 9:38 AM

=============================================================================
PAST MEDICAL HISTORY
=============================================================================
1. Atrial Fibrillation (paroxysmal) — NOT on anticoagulation (patient declined due to fall risk concerns)
2. Hypertension — on Amlodipine 10 mg + Losartan 100 mg
3. Type 2 Diabetes — on Sitagliptin 100 mg daily
4. Hyperlipidemia — on Rosuvastatin 20 mg
5. Prior TIA (2019) — full workup at that time, aspirin started but stopped by patient
6. Moderate obesity (BMI 29.8)

=============================================================================
PHYSICAL EXAMINATION ON ARRIVAL
=============================================================================
Vital Signs:
  Blood Pressure: 168/92 mmHg (permissive hypertension per stroke protocol)
  Heart Rate: 88 bpm (irregular — known AF)
  Respiratory Rate: 16 breaths/min
  Temperature: 36.9 C
  SpO2: 97% on room air
  Blood Glucose (POC): 142 mg/dL

NIHSS Score: 18 (severe stroke)
  Level of consciousness: 1 (drowsy but arousable)
  Gaze deviation: 2 (forced deviation right)
  Visual fields: 1 (partial hemianopia)
  Facial palsy: 2 (complete left facial palsy)
  Left arm motor: 4 (no movement against gravity)
  Left leg motor: 3 (some effort against gravity)
  Limb ataxia: 0
  Sensory: 1 (partial loss left side)
  Language: 1 (mild aphasia)
  Dysarthria: 2 (severe slurring)
  Extinction/Inattention: 1 (left-sided neglect)

=============================================================================
DIAGNOSTIC IMAGING
=============================================================================

Non-Contrast CT Brain (completed at 9:44 AM — 6 minutes after arrival):
  - No acute hemorrhage
  - ASPECTS score: 9/10 (only 1 segment — M3 — shows subtle early ischemic changes)
  - No large established infarct core
  - Hyperdense right MCA sign (thrombus visible in proximal right MCA)
  Interpretation: Acute ischemic stroke, no hemorrhage, favorable imaging for intervention

CT Angiography (CTA) Brain and Neck (completed 9:48 AM):
  - Complete occlusion of right M1 segment of middle cerebral artery
  - Carotid arteries: 40% stenosis at right carotid bifurcation (not critical)
  - Intracranial anterior and posterior circulation otherwise patent
  - Robust collateral circulation on CT perfusion (RAPID software):
    Ischemic Core (rCBF <30%): 18 mL
    Penumbra (Tmax >6 sec): 112 mL
    Mismatch Ratio: 6.2:1 (excellent — large salvageable tissue)

=============================================================================
TIME METRICS (CRITICAL FOR STROKE OUTCOMES)
=============================================================================
Onset to ED arrival: 24 minutes (excellent)
Door to CT completion: 6 minutes
Door to CTA completion: 10 minutes
Door to IV tPA decision: 14 minutes
IV tPA administration (door-to-needle): 22 minutes
CT to groin puncture (thrombectomy): 48 minutes
Groin puncture to recanalization: 34 minutes
Door to recanalization: 82 minutes

(American Heart Association target: door-to-needle <60 min; door-to-recanalization <90 min — BOTH MET)

=============================================================================
TREATMENT
=============================================================================

IV Thrombolysis (tPA):
  Alteplase (tPA) 0.9 mg/kg (max 90 mg) administered at 10:00 AM
  10% bolus over 1 minute, remainder over 60 minutes
  Eligibility confirmed: symptom onset <4.5 hours, no hemorrhage on CT, no absolute contraindications
  Blood pressure maintained <185/110 during tPA administration with IV labetalol

Mechanical Thrombectomy (Endovascular Treatment):
  Patient was simultaneously prepared for thrombectomy (bridging tPA approach):
  Angiography Suite: Patient arrived 10:26 AM

  Procedural details:
    Approach: Right femoral artery access
    Guide catheter: 8F Neuron Max 088 placed in right ICA
    Thrombus location: Right M1 occlusion, confirmed on diagnostic angiogram
    Device: Solitaire X 4 x 40 mm stent retriever
    Technique: ADAPT (A Direct Aspiration First Pass Technique) attempted first; combined with stent retriever
    Pass 1: Partial recanalization; TICI 2a
    Pass 2: Complete recanalization achieved; TICI 3 (near complete, <10% residual stenosis)
    Total passes: 2
    Time to recanalization: 34 minutes after groin puncture
    Complications: None

=============================================================================
POST-PROCEDURE COURSE
=============================================================================

Immediate Post-Procedure (Stroke ICU):
  Repeat CT at 24 hours: No hemorrhagic transformation; established right MCA territory infarction in 24 mL area (smaller than expected, consistent with successful reperfusion)
  NIHSS at 24 hours: 7 (improved from 18; dramatic improvement)
  Blood pressure management: Target <140/90 after first 24 hours
  Anticoagulation: Held first 24 hours; apixaban initiated at 48 hours (AF — high cardioembolic risk)
  Statin intensification: Rosuvastatin increased to 40 mg
  DVT prophylaxis: Sequential compression devices; heparin SQ added at 48 hours

Cardiology Consultation:
  Echocardiogram: Left atrial enlargement; no thrombus; moderate LV hypertrophy; EF 58%
  Long-term cardiac monitoring: Implantable loop recorder (ILR) placed on Day 5 for AF monitoring
  Anticoagulation: Apixaban 5 mg twice daily (long-term for AF stroke prevention)

Neurological Improvement:
  NIHSS at 72 hours: 5 (continued improvement)
  Day 7: Patient ambulating with minimal assistance; speech nearly normal; left arm strength recovering
  Day 10: NIHSS 3 (minor residual left arm weakness and mild neglect)
  mRS (modified Rankin Scale) at discharge: 2 (slight disability but independent in most activities)

=============================================================================
REHABILITATION
=============================================================================
Inpatient rehabilitation: 12 days (Days 10-22 post-admission)
Disciplines: Physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech-language pathology, neuropsychology

Day 22 Assessment:
  Left arm strength: 4+/5
  Ambulation: Independent
  Speech: Near-normal; mild word-finding difficulty
  mRS at discharge from rehab: 1 (no significant disability)

=============================================================================
SECONDARY PREVENTION
=============================================================================
Cause identified: Cardioembolic (atrial fibrillation — thrombus from LAA)

Long-term management:
  - Apixaban 5 mg twice daily (anticoagulation for AF — CHA2DS2-VASc score: 6)
  - Rosuvastatin 40 mg nightly
  - Antihypertensives continued with target BP <130/80
  - Diabetes management continued
  - Lifestyle: Mediterranean diet, moderate exercise, weight management
  - Monitoring: ILR for AF burden monitoring; clinic follow-up quarterly x 1 year, then annually

=============================================================================
DISCUSSION
=============================================================================
This case illustrates the extraordinary impact of timely stroke intervention. With an NIHSS of 18 at presentation (severe stroke), the patient achieved an mRS of 1 at rehabilitation discharge — an exceptional outcome attributable to:

1. Rapid symptom recognition and 911 activation by bystander
2. Pre-hospital stroke scale by EMS enabling pre-notification and team mobilization
3. Organized "stroke code" system at the comprehensive stroke center
4. Excellent time metrics — door-to-needle 22 min, door-to-recanalization 82 min
5. Favorable imaging profile (large penumbra, small core, ASPECTS 9/10)
6. Successful 2-pass thrombectomy achieving TICI 3 recanalization
7. Simultaneous bridging IV tPA

The case also highlights atrial fibrillation as the stroke etiology — the most common modifiable cause of cardioembolic stroke. His prior TIA in 2019 represented a missed opportunity for stroke prevention through anticoagulation. Had the patient accepted anticoagulation after his TIA, this stroke — and its associated suffering and healthcare costs — may have been entirely prevented.

=============================================================================
OUTCOME
=============================================================================
Excellent functional recovery. mRS 1 at 90 days (independent, returned to normal activities). Patient now on appropriate anticoagulation. Stroke risk significantly reduced.
