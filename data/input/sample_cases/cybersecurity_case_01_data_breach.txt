INCIDENT REPORT — CYBERSECURITY / DATA BREACH
Case Reference: IR-2023-0047
Incident Title: Ransomware Attack and Data Exfiltration at Quantum Financial Services, Inc.
Incident Discovery Date: June 14, 2023
Reporting Entity: Quantum Financial Services, Inc. (mandatory breach notification)
Regulatory Notifications: SEC Form 8-K (material cybersecurity incident); state breach notification (47 states)

=============================================================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
=============================================================================
On June 14, 2023, Quantum Financial Services, Inc. ("Quantum"), a regional investment advisory and wealth management firm managing $4.2 billion in assets under management, detected a ransomware attack that had encrypted approximately 60% of the company's internal file servers and exfiltrated sensitive client data including personally identifiable information (PII), financial account data, and Social Security numbers of approximately 184,000 current and former clients.

The attack, attributed to the LockBit 3.0 ransomware-as-a-service group, caused a 6-day total system outage and resulted in an $8.4 million ransom demand. Quantum declined to pay the ransom, engaged law enforcement (FBI Cyber Division) and a cybersecurity incident response firm, and ultimately restored systems from backups within 14 days. Total incident costs (remediation, legal, regulatory, credit monitoring, and business interruption) exceeded $22 million.

TIMELINE OF THE INCIDENT
=============================================================================

Initial Compromise (May 28, 2023 — "Day 0"):
  The threat actor gained initial access through a phishing email sent to a mid-level accounting associate (Emily Torres, accounts payable department). The email purported to be an invoice from an existing vendor (domain spoofing attack: "accounts@acme-vendors-llc.com" vs. legitimate "accounts@acme-vendors.com"). Ms. Torres clicked a malicious link and entered her corporate credentials on a credential harvesting page.

Lateral Movement (May 28 – June 8, 2023):
  Using Ms. Torres's credentials, the threat actor accessed the Quantum corporate VPN. Over the next 11 days, the attacker:
  - Conducted reconnaissance using legitimate Windows tools (Living-off-the-Land technique): net.exe, ipconfig, whoami, nltest
  - Escalated privileges by exploiting CVE-2021-34527 ("PrintNightmare" vulnerability — unpatched in 3 Quantum servers)
  - Obtained Domain Administrator credentials from the Active Directory
  - Mapped the network and identified high-value file servers (client data, financial records)
  - Disabled Windows Defender and third-party AV on targeted systems
  - Established persistent access through multiple back doors including a web shell and scheduled task

Data Exfiltration (June 8-12, 2023):
  Using the Rclone data exfiltration tool (installed under the name "svchost32.exe" to evade detection), the threat actor exfiltrated approximately 2.3 TB of data to a cloud storage service over 4 days:
  - Client personal data (names, addresses, SSNs, DOBs): 184,000 records
  - Financial account information (account numbers, balances, transaction history): 184,000 records
  - Internal financial and business records
  - Employee HR records (640 employees)
  - Proprietary investment strategies and client communications

Ransomware Deployment (June 13, 2023, 2:17 AM):
  At 2:17 AM, the threat actor deployed LockBit 3.0 ransomware binary simultaneously across the compromised systems. The ransomware:
  - Encrypted files on 47 of 80 file servers (using AES-256 encryption with RSA-4096 key)
  - Deleted Volume Shadow Copies (backup mechanism) on affected systems
  - Dropped ransom note: "!!QUANTUM_DECRYPT_READ!!.txt" on all affected desktops
  - Ransom demand: 150 Bitcoin (~$8.4 million at June 14 prices) within 72 hours or threatened public data release

Discovery (June 14, 2023, 7:33 AM):
  A network administrator detected the attack when multiple help desk calls reported encrypted files and ransom notes on desktop screens. Incident response was immediately activated.

=============================================================================
INCIDENT RESPONSE
=============================================================================

Immediate Containment (Day 1 — June 14):
  - Emergency call to incident response retainer firm: CyberShield Response Group (CSG)
  - Network isolation: All affected servers taken offline; VPN disabled for all employees
  - FBI Cyber Division notified (per company policy; victim company cooperation with law enforcement)
  - Crisis management team convened: CEO, CTO, General Counsel, CISO, IR firm
  - Business continuity plan activated: Essential operations shifted to paper-based processes and backup systems

Forensic Investigation (Days 1-14):
  CSG deployed 12-person incident response team:
  - Forensic images captured from all affected and potentially affected systems
  - Timeline reconstruction using Windows Event Logs, firewall logs, SIEM data
  - Identified complete attack chain from initial phishing to ransomware deployment
  - Confirmed scope of exfiltration via DNS logs, firewall logs, and cloud storage provider cooperation

Initial Attribution:
  LockBit 3.0 ransomware variant confirmed by malware analysis. FBI shared intelligence corroborating LockBit attribution. Specific affiliate group suspected based on TTP comparison (not publicly disclosed to protect investigation).

Ransom Decision:
  Executive team and Board, advised by legal counsel and FBI:
  Decision: DECLINE TO PAY ransom
  Rationale: (1) No guarantee data would not be released even after payment; (2) FBI advised against payment (emboldens attackers, potential sanctions risk if actor is sanctioned entity); (3) Backups available for restoration; (4) Payment could violate OFAC sanctions regulations

Data Recovery (Days 3-14):
  IT team and CSG worked in parallel to:
  - Restore from offline backups (last clean backup: June 9 — 4 days of operational data lost)
  - Rebuild 3 servers for which backups were incomplete
  - Reimage and reconstitute client data records by cross-referencing with custodian banks
  - Full system restoration completed Day 14

BREACH ANALYSIS AND SCOPE
=============================================================================

Data Compromised:
  Category A — Client PII: 184,000 individuals
    Data elements: Full name, address, date of birth, Social Security number, email, phone number
    Sensitivity: HIGH — SSN exposure creates significant identity theft risk

  Category B — Financial Account Data: 184,000 accounts
    Data elements: Account numbers, brokerage balances, transaction history, investment positions
    Sensitivity: HIGH — financial account takeover risk; privacy violations

  Category C — Employee HR Data: 640 employees
    Data elements: Names, SSNs, addresses, salary information, performance reviews
    Sensitivity: HIGH

  Category D — Proprietary Business Data: Investment strategies, client communications, internal financials
    Sensitivity: MEDIUM-HIGH (business competitive harm; client fiduciary duty implications)

Root Cause Analysis:
  Primary cause: Successful phishing leading to credential theft
  Contributing factors:
  1. Unpatched critical vulnerability (PrintNightmare, CVE-2021-34527) — patch available 2 years prior
  2. Insufficient MFA enforcement (VPN accessed with single-factor credentials)
  3. Excessive user privileges (accounting associate had access to file servers outside her role)
  4. Inadequate email security (domain spoofing not caught by email security gateway)
  5. SIEM alerts not reviewed in timely manner (automated alert on unusual login time generated Day 1 but not actioned for 3 days)
  6. Volume Shadow Copy deletion not monitored or prevented
  7. No network segmentation between accounting department and sensitive file servers

=============================================================================
REMEDIATION AND SECURITY IMPROVEMENTS
=============================================================================

Immediate (Completed within 90 days):
  - MFA enforced on ALL systems including VPN, email, and all internal applications
  - Critical vulnerability patching: Emergency patching program implemented; all critical CVEs now patched within 72 hours
  - Email security enhancement: DMARC/DKIM/SPF implemented; anti-phishing AI tools deployed
  - Privileged access management (PAM) solution implemented
  - Network segmentation: Finance/accounting network isolated from client data servers
  - Enhanced SIEM monitoring: 24/7 SOC engagement with third-party managed security service provider (MSSP)
  - Endpoint detection and response (EDR): CrowdStrike Falcon deployed enterprise-wide
  - Volume Shadow Copy protection: Ransomware-aware backup solution implemented

Long-Term (12-18 months):
  - Zero-trust network architecture implementation
  - Enhanced security awareness training with phishing simulation program
  - Tabletop incident response exercises (quarterly)
  - Third-party penetration testing (annual)
  - Supply chain security assessment (all vendors with system access)
  - Cyber insurance policy increased to $25 million

=============================================================================
LESSONS LEARNED
=============================================================================

1. Phishing Remains the Primary Entry Vector:
   Despite industry-wide awareness, credential phishing remains the most common initial access technique. Technical controls (MFA, email authentication) are more reliable than user behavior change alone.

2. Dwell Time Creates Catastrophic Damage:
   The 17-day gap between initial compromise and ransomware deployment represents the "dwell time" during which the attacker moved laterally and exfiltrated data before triggering the destructive phase. Early detection during this window is where the greatest loss reduction opportunity lies.

3. Patch Management is Non-Negotiable:
   The PrintNightmare vulnerability was over 2 years old with a publicly available patch. Unpatched systems remain the easiest path for attackers to escalate privileges.

4. Backups Must Be Immutable and Offline:
   Volume Shadow Copy deletion is standard ransomware behavior. Backups accessible from the production network will be targeted. Immutable, air-gapped backups are essential for recovery without ransom payment.

5. Incident Response Retainer Value:
   The pre-established incident response retainer enabled engagement within 2 hours of breach discovery. Companies without retainers face 24-48 hour delays in engaging qualified IR firms during peak demand periods.

6. Ransomware is a Data Breach:
   Organizations must treat ransomware attacks as presumptive data breaches requiring full notification protocols. The "we were ransomwared but didn't lose data" defense is rarely tenable after forensic analysis.
